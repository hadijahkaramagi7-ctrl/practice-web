Introduction

Rehoboth Springs Farm is a registered company located in Wakiso Town Council, Wakiso District. I have always taken care of animals including birds, but in 2015 I decided to enroll for the Artisan Certificate in Dairy Industry and Business at Makerere University to acquire skills and learn how to professionally take care of animals. In addition, I wanted to have an alternative family source of income.

When I came to AFRISA I was taught how to do dairy farming as a business. This opened my eyes, and I took keen interest in the dairy value chain. We were taught how to process yoghurt and cheese.

In January 2019, something transformational happened. Originally, we were selling milk to the neighbours at UGX 1,400 per litre. The money could not maintain the cows and the farm. I started the idea of churning milk and selling ghee. I did not only get ghee, but I also got other byproducts like Amacunda (Cultured Buttermilk).

Which we have now re-branded Fat-Free Cunda Yoghurt (cultured buttermilk), Eshabwe and Amaranga (whey).

Preserving the cultural aroma

Many people from western Uganda treasure milk, amakamo, amacunda and ghee, which have been kept in smoked gourds. The ghee, amacunda and amakamo are all processed and kept in smoked gourds known as ebishaabo, ebirere and entsimbo.

One litre, five products
1

Fresh milk

Milk from the farm’s cows, collected for processing rather than sold raw.

2

Churning

The milk is churned. The ghee settles and is removed from the liquid.

3

Ghee

Healthy fats with Vitamin A, E and D — also spiced into ready-to-eat appetizer.

4

Amacunda

The liquid left behind, re-branded as Fat-Free Cunda Yoghurt. Ghee also becomes eshabwe.

5

Amaranga

The last by-product, whey — sold to pig farmers and used in different cuisines.

Our products

Fat-Free Yoghurt Cunda (Cultured Buttermilk)

This product is produced after churning. Ghee settles and is removed. The liquid that remains is Fat-Free yoghurt. This improved traditional milk is good for both the young and old people. The protein found in this milk is called whey. It is more nutritious and has more bioactive compounds than the casein (solid protein). Whey contains all the essential amino acids in the proper proportions, making it an excellent source of nutrition for therapeutic and supplementary foods.

Protein

This drink is cherished by the youth who do body building who need it to tone their muscles and for persons interested in loosing excess body weight.

Ghee

Ghee contains a good amount of healthy fats, Vitamin A, E and D. Many people think that the high content of fat in ghee is bad for health but, in fact, fat is one of the major nutrients that is required by our body to function properly.

Appetizer – Ready to eat ghee

This is a spiced delicacy. Well made for school going children (Boarding schools).

Eshabwe

Eshabwe is a special cuisine got from ghee, which is treasured by people from western Uganda. It is now a treasured delicacy in most parts of the country during special occasions.

Amaranga (Whey)

This is the last by-product in the milk value chain. Although whey has huge potential of turning around the dairy industry, for now we sell it to pig farmers. It has the same nutritional value as amacunda including being used in preparation of different cuisines.

Benefits

The benefits from this milk value chain have been enormous, and they include various milk products to customers, feeds for piggery farmers, creation of jobs, provision of market for the crafts industry, and income to the farm. By adopting value addition, we have been able to earn a gross income of about UGX 7,000 per litre.

Prospects

We envisage to have a factory with modern equipment to fully optimise the processing of these milk products. This will provide milk products for families, a good market for dairy farmers, jobs for youths and women, and support Uganda’s economic growth.
